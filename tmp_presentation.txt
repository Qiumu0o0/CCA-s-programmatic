Problem Setting

  Consider a finite-state system G observed by two agents.

       • Agent A1 estimates the current state of G from its observations.
       • Agent A2 has a different observation of the same execution.
       • Agent A2 infers the possible state estimates held by Agent A1 .

  Models
                                     G = (Q, E , δ, Q0 ),   Ai = (Ei , Σi , ℓi ),
  where Ei ⊆ E and ℓi : E → Σi ∪ {ϵ} maps events outside Ei to ϵ.

  Target computation
  For an observation β of Agent A2 , compute

                       MA1 ←A2 (G, β) = {M1 (G, ℓ1 (s)) : s ∈ L(G), ℓ2 (s) = β}.

Theorem


  If reading β leads the order-2 observer to the state

                                                   {(q1 , X1 ), . . . , (qn , Xn )},

  then
                                             MA1 ←A2 (G, β) = {X1 , . . . , Xn }.

  Each pair (qi , Xi ) records a possible actual state qi and a corresponding
  estimate Xi held by Agent A1 . Therefore, projecting the second components
  gives Agent A2 ’s inference about Agent A1 .


  Source: Zhang et al., High-Order Observers and High-Order State-Estimation-Based Properties, Theorem 25.

Complexity and Summary


  Let n = |Q|. In the worst case,
                                                                                  n
                        |ObsA1 | ≤ 2n ,         |CC | ≤ n2n ,   |ObsA1 ←A2 | ≤ 2n2 .

  Therefore, explicit construction has a double-exponential upper bound in both
  time and storage, although only reachable states are generated.

  Summary
    • The programs implement LFSA conversion, observers, and concurrent
      composition.
    • The resulting order-2 observer computes Agent A2 ’s possible inferences
      about Agent A1 .
    • The published example provides an end-to-end validation case.
